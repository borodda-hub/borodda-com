## Research profile

My research and creative practice investigate how design mediates between computational systems and human experience. Working across data visualization, experiential and immersive media, game studies, and urban computing, I examine how complex systems — cities, sports environments, decision contexts, and play — can be made legible and generative through designed interfaces and interactive media. Recent peer-reviewed and in-progress work develops *decision quality* as a portable analytic lens for game studies, theorizes industry-partnered design studios as a vehicle for interdisciplinary higher education, and explores AI-assisted decision coaching. In 2026, as a Strong Research Fellow at The Strong National Museum of Play, I conducted archival and artifact research on Nintendo's R.O.B. and 1980s companion robot toys, the first phase of a broader project on how play media shaped public expectations for robotic and AI companions. A second strand of practice-based research advances data visualization as a humanistic medium, including dynamic urban data platforms developed in collaboration with national laboratories, civic institutions, and major industry partners.
