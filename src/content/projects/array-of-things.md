**What would it mean for a city to have an instrument, the way a laboratory does?**

The Array of Things set out to treat Chicago as something that could be measured continuously and in public: a network of sensor nodes mounted on city streets, producing open data on the urban environment for researchers, city agencies and residents alike. It was led by Charles Catlett at the Urban Center for Computation and Data and funded by the National Science Foundation.

My part in the project was on two sides of the same problem. One was the object: contributing to the design of the node's components and how they would be fabricated. The other was the public: helping shape how an instrument placed in neighborhoods would be explained to the people living beside it. That work included physical-computing workshops for high school students, co-led with Robb Drinkwater and Douglas Pancoast of the School of the Art Institute of Chicago.

The project sits in the [Urban Computing and Smart Cities](/research/urban-computing-and-smart-cities/) strand, between [LakeSIM](/projects/lakesim/), which modeled a city that did not yet exist, and the studios at Illinois Tech that later worked with urban data.
