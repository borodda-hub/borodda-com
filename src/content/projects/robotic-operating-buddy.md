**Long before conversational AI, a generation was sold a robot to play with.**

Nintendo's Robotic Operating Buddy, released in 1985, was a mass-market attempt to put a robot companion beside a child and a television. This project asks what that attempt can tell us: how companion robots were designed, how they were sold to retailers and families, and how they failed as playmates.

The first phase was carried out in 2026 as a Strong Research Fellow at The Strong National Museum of Play in Rochester, New York. The research drew on the International Center for the History of Electronic Games, the Brian Sutton-Smith Library and Archives of Play, and the museum's toy collections, and included hands-on study of R.O.B., Teddy Ruxpin and Tomy Omnibot alongside trade materials and period press.

The challenge is evidentiary. The promise of a companion lives in packaging, advertising and the memory of play, more than in the mechanism. Reading those sources together is the method.

The work is the opening of a longer inquiry into how play media shaped public expectations of robotic and AI companions. A manuscript is in preparation.
