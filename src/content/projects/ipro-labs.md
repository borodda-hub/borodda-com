**What does a university owe the organizations its students work with, and what do they owe the students?**

Illinois Tech's Interprofessional Projects Program places undergraduates from every discipline on shared projects. After five years of leading those studios, the question was structural: how to turn a series of individual courses into something partners could return to and students could build on.

IPRO Labs, co-developed with Limia Shunia and launched in spring 2021, was the answer. It organized the program's foundational course into five thematic labs (CommunityLab, CityLab, EducationLab, GameLab and EnergyLab), each holding a portfolio of industry and community partnerships over time.

The hard part of partnered teaching is that two clocks run at once. A semester ends; a partner's problem does not. The lab structure was designed to let a question outlast a single cohort, and to make the partner relationship part of what students learn.

The studios run under this model include the [Big Monster Toys Lab](/projects/big-monster-toys-lab/), the [IPRO Sports Lab](/projects/ipro-sports-lab/) and [Designing the Future of the Departure Lounge](/projects/departure-lounge/). The thinking continues in the [Industry-Engaged Pedagogy](/research/industry-engaged-pedagogy/) strand.
