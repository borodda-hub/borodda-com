**Most of a journey by air is spent sitting still, in a room no one set out to design.**

The departure lounge is where an airline's operations, an airport's architecture and a traveler's patience meet. This IPRO studio, run in summer 2021 with United Airlines, asked students from across Illinois Tech to treat the time before boarding as a design problem in its own right.

The studio began on a whiteboard and moved to O'Hare. What students brought back was less about furniture than about feeling: anxiety over missed flights, too many signs competing for attention, no consistent way to find anything, and little help for travelers with accessibility needs. From there the teams divided the problem.

One team took the seat. Two rounds of concepts were judged against form, function, comfort and versatility, and converged on a single unit meant to hold what a waiting passenger actually carries and does: privacy, power, a work surface and a place for luggage.

A second team took navigation. Their proposal was a concierge that follows a traveler from a kiosk in the terminal to the phone in their hand, with a route to the gate and to whatever lies along the way. The students named its difficulties themselves: feasibility, cost, durability, and what it means for an airline to know where a passenger is standing.

Two further teams looked past the gate, at a travel plan for students and their families and at the airline's sustainability practices.

The challenge of the studio was scope. A gate area belongs to many owners and answers to safety, schedule and revenue before comfort. The work ended at the concept stage; the teams' own next steps were physical models, a full-scale prototype and testing in the field. Learning to design inside those constraints, with partners who live with them, was the point.

The studio was part of [IPRO Labs](/projects/ipro-labs/).
