**What would a city look like if its map were made of the same glass as its skyline, and lit by its own activity?**

Intermodal is a public art proposal for the Intermodal Transit Terminal at Chicago O'Hare International Airport, developed with Ben Stagl at [ChiLab Design Studio](https://chilabstudio.com). The proposal was not selected. It is shown here as a concept, and for the prototypes made to test it. It takes as its subject the thing visitors first see from the air at night: the Chicago grid, a field of light that runs to the horizon and stops at the lake.

The proposal renders that grid in more than a thousand ladle-cast glass tiles, each one unique, spanning nearly one hundred and fifty feet by thirty. Every tile is an inverted bas-relief of one block of the city's streets, rail lines, waterways and building footprints. Beyond the city limits, the rail and interstate lines continue outward across the wall.

The challenge was to make data a material property and not a screen. Each tile is framed by networked LEDs and addressed individually, so the wall can be driven by the city's public data portal and reprogrammed over the life of the piece. The geometry moves from geographic shape-files to a three-dimensional model, to a milled or printed sand mold, to poured glass.

Prototype tiles were cast and lit in 2016. The work continues the questions of the [Chicago City Model](/projects/chicago-city-model/) at the scale of architecture: how a city's data can be read in public, by people standing in front of it.
