**How many unique parts can a wall have before it can no longer be made?**

The feature wall at Uber Freight's headquarters in the Old Chicago Post Office is a [ChiLab Design Studio](https://chilabstudio.com/work/uber-feature-wall.html) project, designed with Ben Stagl. It takes its pattern from a shipping yard seen from above: thousands of containers, alike in kind and different in position. The design question was how to carry that image into architecture without reducing it to a repeated panel.

The answer was parametric. A model built in Grasshopper generated the pattern to fit the constraints of the space, then divided it into individual cast pieces. Every piece is unique, and every piece belongs to one continuous field.

That is where the difficulty lies. Uniqueness is cheap in a model and expensive in a foundry. The project depended on automating the production of molds so that the number of distinct parts stayed manageable, and on a sequence of finishing and site assembly that brought the castings back into a single surface.

The wall belongs with the [Grand Spiral Stair](/projects/grand-spiral-stair/), made for the same building, as a study in what digital design contributes when it stays in conversation with casting, finishing and installation.
